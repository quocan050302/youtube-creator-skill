Touch a magnet to a copper pipe—nothing sticks. Drop it inside, and why does it crawl down in slow motion? Drop an unmagnetized steel bolt the same size, and it hits the table instantly. Copper isn't ferromagnetic, so nothing pulls on a still magnet. The brake only appears once it moves. As the falling magnet's field sweeps down the conductive copper, it induces swirling electric loops called eddy currents. By Lenz's law, those currents generate an opposing magnetic field that pushes back against the fall. Think of dragging a paddle through water—faster push, stronger pushback—except here there's no fluid, just electromagnetism. Roller coasters use this exact contactless braking to slow cars with zero brake pads wearing out.
